# Executive Briefing for Policymaker: Accelerating India's Transition to Electric Vehicles

## Headline
India's path to reducing oil dependency and enhancing energy security lies in accelerating the adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) through strategic investments and policy adjustments tailored to its unique market dynamics.

## Situation
As of 2026, India's EV market is growing, with a sales share of 6.2% [unverified]. However, the country faces challenges in scaling up its EV infrastructure and supply chain, with only 67,657 public charging points available ([source](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/plugging-gap-why-67000-ev-charging-points-arent-enough-for-indias-electric-dreams/articleshow/134198422.cms)). The current policy framework includes purchase subsidies, tax exemptions, and reduced import duties to encourage EV adoption ([source](https://e-amrit.niti.gov.in/electric-vehicle-incentives)).

## Prioritized Recommendations

1. **Expand Charging Infrastructure**
   - **Impact**: High. Increasing the number of charging stations will directly support EV adoption by reducing range anxiety.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment). While initial investments are significant, they are crucial for long-term EV market growth.
   - **Timeline**: 2-3 years. Accelerate deployment in urban centers and along highways.

2. **Promote Battery Swapping for Two-Wheelers**
   - **Impact**: High. Given the dominance of two-wheelers in India's vehicle market, battery swapping can significantly enhance convenience and reduce downtime.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment). Requires less infrastructure than traditional charging stations.
   - **Timeline**: 1-2 years. Leverage existing networks and partnerships with local businesses.

3. **Enhance Domestic Battery Manufacturing and Recycling**
   - **Impact**: High. Reducing reliance on imports for battery components will mitigate supply chain risks.
   - **Cost**: High (judgment). Requires substantial investment in technology and infrastructure.
   - **Timeline**: 3-5 years. Focus on establishing partnerships with global leaders in battery technology.

4. **Strengthen Incentives for Fleet Electrification**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Targeting commercial fleets can lead to substantial oil displacement and emissions reduction.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment). Incentives can be structured to minimize fiscal impact while maximizing uptake.
   - **Timeline**: 2-4 years. Align with existing fleet turnover cycles.

5. **Develop Second-Life Battery Markets**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Utilizing retired EV batteries for energy storage can enhance grid stability and sustainability.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment). Leverages existing battery technology with minimal additional investment.
   - **Timeline**: 1-3 years. Initiate pilot projects to demonstrate feasibility and benefits.

## Oil-Displacement and CO2 Impact
- **MODEL ESTIMATE**: Achieving 30% fleet electrification for two-wheelers could displace 272,939 barrels/day, avoiding $6.93 billion in import costs annually, and reduce CO2 emissions by 21.47 million tonnes/year.
- **Assumptions**: 30.0% of the two-wheeler fleet (90,000,000 vehicles), 8,000 km/year, 2.2 L/100km, 3 kWh/100km, oil $80/bbl, oil import share 87%, grid 700 gCO2/kWh.

## Supply Chain Risks
India faces significant supply chain risks due to high concentration in critical mineral refining, particularly lithium and cobalt, dominated by China ([source](https://discoveryalert.com.au/critical-mineral-dependencies-global-battery-supply-chain-2026/)). Domestic refining capacity is minimal, increasing vulnerability to global market fluctuations ([source](https://ieefa.org/resources/securing-indias-battery-supply-chain-more-critical-ever)).

## 12-Month Watchlist

1. **Progress on Charging Infrastructure Deployment**: Monitor the expansion of charging stations in key urban areas and highways.
2. **Battery Swapping Initiatives**: Track the rollout and adoption rates of battery swapping stations for two-wheelers.
3. **Domestic Battery Manufacturing Developments**: Observe investments and partnerships in local battery production facilities.
4. **Policy Adjustments on Fleet Electrification**: Evaluate the effectiveness of incentives and mandates for commercial fleet electrification.
5. **China's Export Policies**: Stay informed on any changes in China's export controls on critical minerals, which could impact India's battery supply chain.

By focusing on these strategic areas, India can accelerate its transition to electric vehicles, reduce oil dependency, and enhance energy security.
