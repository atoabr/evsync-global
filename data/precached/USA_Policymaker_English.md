# Executive Briefing: Accelerating the Transition from Oil in the United States

## Headline
"Strategic Pathways to Enhance Energy Security and Reduce Oil Dependency in the United States"

## Situation
As of 2026, the United States is at a pivotal point in its transition to electric vehicles (EVs), with a current EV sales share of 10% of all vehicles [IEA Global EV Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/executive-summary). Despite significant federal and state incentives, such as tax credits and rebates, the pace of adoption is constrained by supply chain vulnerabilities and infrastructure gaps. The U.S. is a net energy exporter, with net energy imports at -9% of use [World Bank Net Energy Imports](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.IMP.CONS.ZS?locations=US), yet it remains reliant on imports for critical battery materials, posing risks to energy security.

## Prioritized Recommendations

1. **Expand Domestic Battery Material Processing**
   - **Impact**: High. Reduces reliance on imports and enhances energy security.
   - **Cost**: High (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (3-5 years).
   - **Rationale**: The U.S. is heavily reliant on countries like China for refining critical minerals such as lithium and cobalt [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/document/securing-the-critical-battery-materials-supply-chain-in-the-southeast-united-states/). Expanding domestic processing capabilities will mitigate geopolitical risks and stabilize supply chains.

2. **Enhance EV Charging Infrastructure**
   - **Impact**: High. Facilitates EV adoption by reducing range anxiety.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years).
   - **Rationale**: With 253,319 public charging ports available [Fuel Cells Works](https://fuelcellsworks.com/2026/06/22/battery/us-reaches-250-000-public-ev-charging-ports), further expansion is needed to support growing EV numbers and ensure equitable access across regions.

3. **Promote Battery Recycling Initiatives**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Reduces dependency on raw material imports and promotes sustainability.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (3-5 years).
   - **Rationale**: Investing in recycling technologies can create a circular economy for battery materials, reducing waste and import needs [Energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/edf/articles/sector-spotlight-critical-materials).

4. **Implement Battery Swapping Stations**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Provides a quick alternative to charging, especially for urban areas.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years).
   - **Rationale**: Battery swapping can alleviate range anxiety and reduce downtime for EV users, enhancing the attractiveness of EVs [Columbia University](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/publications/the-ira-and-the-us-battery-supply-chain-background-and-key-drivers/).

5. **Strengthen Trade Policies for Critical Minerals**
   - **Impact**: High. Ensures stable supply chains and reduces geopolitical risks.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Immediate (within 1 year).
   - **Rationale**: Current export restrictions on critical minerals could impact market dynamics [MSCI](https://www.msci.org/u-s-commerce-department-to-have-power-to-restrict-critical-minerals-exports/). Strengthening trade policies will secure necessary imports and support domestic industry growth.

## Oil-Displacement and CO2 Impact
- **MODEL ESTIMATE**: At 10% fleet electrification, oil displacement is 578,961 barrels per day, with CO2 avoided at 52,752,000 tonnes per year. At 30% electrification, displacement increases to 1,736,883 barrels per day, with CO2 avoided at 158,256,000 tonnes per year.
- **Assumptions**: 10.0% of Car fleet (28,000,000 vehicles), 15,000 km/year, 8.0 L/100km, 16 kWh/100km, oil $80/bbl, oil import share 0%, grid 370 gCO2/kWh.

## Supply Chain Risks
- **High Concentration Risk**: The U.S. relies on a few countries for critical minerals, with China dominating refining processes (e.g., 90% of graphite refining) [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/document/securing-the-critical-battery-materials-supply-chain-in-the-southeast-united-states/).
- **Export Restrictions**: New regulations may limit the ability to export critical minerals, impacting market dynamics [MSCI](https://www.msci.org/u-s-commerce-department-to-have-power-to-restrict-critical-minerals-exports/).

## 12-Month Watchlist
1. **Federal Policy Changes**: Monitor for any new federal incentives or regulatory changes impacting EV adoption.
2. **Battery Material Prices**: Track fluctuations in global prices for lithium, cobalt, and nickel.
3. **EV Sales Trends**: Observe shifts in consumer preferences and sales data for EVs.
4. **Infrastructure Developments**: Follow progress in public and private investments in EV charging infrastructure.
5. **Geopolitical Developments**: Stay informed on international relations affecting critical mineral supply chains.
