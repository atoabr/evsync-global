# Executive Briefing: Accelerating the UK's Transition from Oil

## Headline
Accelerating the UK's Transition from Oil: Strategic Recommendations for a Secure and Sustainable Energy Future

## Situation
The United Kingdom is at a pivotal moment in its transition to electric vehicles (EVs), with a 41.4% share of new car sales being battery electric vehicles (BEVs) as of 2026 [source](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/07/24/evs-at-41-4-share-in-the-uk-tesla-leading-bev-brand/). This exceeds the current Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV) mandate target of 33% for 2026 [source](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/uk-government-launches-review-into-ev-mandate-for-automakers/827986/). The UK government has set ambitious targets to increase this to 80% by 2030 and 100% by 2035. However, challenges such as high petrol prices (174.13p per litre) [weak source] and electricity prices (26.32p per kWh) [weak source] persist, alongside supply chain vulnerabilities in critical minerals.

## Recommendations
1. **Enhance Domestic Battery Recycling Initiatives**
   - **Impact**: High. Reduces reliance on imported critical minerals and supports a circular economy.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (2-3 years).
   - **Rationale**: Expanding initiatives like ReLiB can mitigate supply chain risks and reduce environmental impact [source](https://watt-logic.com/2024/01/11/critical-minerals-for-the-energy-transition-lithium-cobalt-and-nickel/).

2. **Invest in Public Charging Infrastructure**
   - **Impact**: High. Supports EV adoption by addressing range anxiety.
   - **Cost**: High (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years).
   - **Rationale**: Despite having 119,080 public charging points [source](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-april-2026/public-electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-april-2026), further expansion is needed to meet growing demand.

3. **Promote Battery Swapping and Second-Life Use Technologies**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Enhances sustainability and reduces waste.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (2-3 years).
   - **Rationale**: These technologies can extend the life of EV batteries and reduce costs [weak source].

4. **Strengthen Domestic Mining and Refining Capabilities**
   - **Impact**: High. Reduces dependence on foreign sources for critical minerals.
   - **Cost**: High (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Long-term (3-5 years).
   - **Rationale**: Developing domestic capabilities can mitigate risks associated with high concentration in countries like China and DR Congo [source](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-battery-strategy/uk-battery-strategy-html-version).

5. **Review and Adjust EV Incentives and Mandates**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Ensures alignment with market conditions and technological advancements.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment).
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1 year).
   - **Rationale**: Regular reviews can ensure that incentives remain effective and mandates are achievable [source](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/uk-government-launches-review-into-ev-mandate-for-automakers/827986/).

## Oil-Displacement and CO2 Impact
- **MODEL ESTIMATE**: At 10% fleet electrification, the UK could displace 74,438 barrels of oil per day, avoiding an import bill of $1.3 billion annually and reducing CO2 emissions by 8.25 million tonnes per year.
- **Assumptions**: 10.0% of Car fleet (3,600,000 vehicles), 15,000 km/year, 8.0 L/100km, 16 kWh/100km, oil $80/bbl, oil import share 60%, grid 200 gCO2/kWh.

## Supply Chain Risks
1. **High Concentration in Critical Minerals**: Lithium, cobalt, and graphite refining are highly concentrated in China, posing a significant supply chain risk [unverified].
2. **Export Restrictions**: Increasing export restrictions on critical minerals could disrupt supply chains [source](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/august/export-restrictions-imposed-on-some-critical-minerals).
