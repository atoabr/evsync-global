# Executive Briefing: Accelerating Indonesia's Transition from Oil

## 1. Headline
Indonesia is poised to accelerate its transition from oil dependency by leveraging its robust nickel resources, expanding electric vehicle (EV) adoption, and enhancing local manufacturing capabilities.

## 2. Situation
As of 2026, Indonesia's EV market is growing, with an 18.3% sales share for electric vehicles, primarily driven by government subsidies for electric motorbikes and tax incentives for battery electric vehicles (BEVs) [Focus2Move](https://www.focus2move.com/indonesian-vehicles-sales/). The country is also a significant player in the global nickel market, which is crucial for battery production [Global Battery](https://www.globalbattery.org/blog/indonesias-battery-ambition-can-industrial-growth-and-esg-leadership-go-hand-in-hand/). However, Indonesia faces challenges such as high net energy imports (45% of use) [World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IDN/indicator/EG.IMP.CONS.ZS) and dependency on foreign refining and manufacturing, particularly from China.

## 3. Prioritized Recommendations

1. **Expand Local Battery Manufacturing**
   - **Impact**: High. Reduces dependency on imports and strengthens local supply chains.
   - **Cost**: High (judgment). Requires significant investment in infrastructure and technology.
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (3-5 years). Aligns with existing plans to develop an integrated value chain for nickel refining and battery production [Global Battery](https://www.globalbattery.org/blog/indonesias-battery-ambition-can-industrial-growth-and-esg-leadership-go-hand-in-hand/).

2. **Enhance Charging Infrastructure**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Supports EV adoption by addressing range anxiety.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment). Infrastructure expansion requires moderate investment.
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years). Builds on current government efforts to expand charging points [ICCT Roadmap](https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ID-514-%E2%80%93-IDN-roadmap-2026_working-paper_final.pdf).

3. **Implement Battery Recycling Programs**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Reduces environmental impact and enhances resource efficiency.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment). Utilizes existing waste management frameworks.
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years). Leverages opportunities in battery recycling and second-life use [Climateworks](https://climateworkscentre.org/news/whats-next-for-indonesias-battery-ambitions-key-insights-from-climateworks-latest-analysis/).

4. **Promote Domestic EV Manufacturing**
   - **Impact**: High. Boosts local employment and reduces import dependency.
   - **Cost**: Medium (judgment). Requires incentives for local manufacturers.
   - **Timeline**: Medium-term (3-5 years). Supports the shift from import duties to local production [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/read/2048323/indonesia-wont-extend-ev-import-incentives-in-2026).

5. **Strengthen Regional Policy Coordination**
   - **Impact**: Medium. Ensures consistent policy implementation across regions.
   - **Cost**: Low (judgment). Involves administrative coordination and policy alignment.
   - **Timeline**: Short-term (1-2 years). Addresses the shift of tax incentives control to regional governments [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/indonesia-ends-nationwide-ev-tax-breaks-shifts-control-to-regions/).

## 4. Oil-Displacement and CO2 Impact
- **MODEL ESTIMATE**: Electrifying 10% of the two-wheeler fleet could displace 45,490 barrels of oil per day, avoiding $597.7 million in import bills annually, and reducing CO2 emissions by 3.65 million tonnes per year.
- **Assumptions**: 10% of 2-wheeler fleet (15,000,000 vehicles), 8,000 km/year, 2.2 L/100km, 3 kWh/100km, oil $80/bbl, oil import share 45%, grid 680 gCO2/kWh.

## 5. Supply Chain Risks
- **High Concentration in Cobalt and Graphite**: Refining is dominated by China, posing a supply risk [unverified].
- **Nickel Export Restrictions**: May limit market access and affect global supply chains [Indonesia's Ban on Raw Nickel Exports](https://cetex.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Indonesia-Ban-on-Exports-of-Raw-Nickel.pdf).

## 6. 12-Month Watchlist
1. **Progress on Charging Infrastructure Expansion**: Monitor government initiatives and private sector partnerships.
2. **Development of Local Battery Manufacturing**: Track investments and policy support for domestic production.
3. **Implementation of Regional Tax Incentives**: Assess regional governments' effectiveness in promoting EV adoption.
4. **Nickel Export Policy Changes**: Watch for regulatory updates affecting nickel export and refining.
5. **EV Adoption Rates**: Evaluate the impact of subsidies and incentives on EV sales growth.

This briefing outlines a strategic path for Indonesia to reduce its oil dependency while capitalizing on its natural resources and growing EV market.
